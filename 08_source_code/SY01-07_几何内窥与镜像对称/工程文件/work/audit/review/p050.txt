When we use Hitchin’s equations (which in this rank 1 case reduce to ordinary Hodge
theory) to relate the pair (D, L) to a rank 1 local system E → Y , the absolute value
of the monodromy at ui is exp(−2πIm σi∗ ). The vanishing of the sum of the residues
maps to the fact that for a GL1 local system, the product of these absolute values is 1.
(The phases of the monodromies are determined by α∗i .)
   Now let us consider the case that X = Te∗ Y is actually an affine deformation of T ∗ Y .
In this case, at each singular point ui , we can still define a residue σi∗ ; indeed, the affine
deformation is trivial locally, and though there is no canonical way to trivialize it, a
change in the trivialization does not affect the residue. What is new in the case of an
affine deformation is that the sum of residues no longer vanishes. Instead, we have
                                      X
(4.20)                                     σi∗ − ζC = 0.

(One way to derive this result is to observe that we could trivialize ζ away from an
arbitrarily chosen point r ∈ Y . Relative to this trivialization, D has one more singular
point, with residue −ζC , and eqn. (4.20) is simply the vanishing of the sum of all
residues.) The fact that the sum of the residues is a nonzero constant corresponds to
the fact that the product of the absolute values of the monodromies is a constant not
equal to 1, in fact equal to exp(2πIm ζC ).
   This resolves the puzzle mentioned at the end of Section 4.5. In that computation,
we used coordinates z on Y = M and v on the fiber of Te∗ M. The choice of these
coordinates trivialized the affine deformation except at z = ∞. In particular, the
deformation was trivialized near the poles of D and hence did not affect the residues of
those poles. With this trivialization, D has an additional pole at z = ∞, and of course,
the sum of all residues, including that last one, vanishes.

a meromorphic differential ω. Rather it corresponds to a (1, 0)-form on Y , with poles
as above, and obeying

定理，留数之和为零：

[begin equation]
(4.19)
\sum_i\sigma_i^*=0.

[end equation]

当我们用 Hitchin 方程（在这个秩 1 情形它们约化为通常的 Hodge 理论）把对 $(D,\mathcal{L})$ 与 $Y$ 上秩 1 局部系统 $E$ 联系起来时，$u_i$ 处单值的绝对值为 $\exp(-2\pi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma_i^*)$。留数之和为零反映为：对 $GL_1$ 局部系统，这些绝对值之积为 1。（单值的相位由 $\alpha_i^*$ 决定。）

现在考察 $X=\widetilde{T}^*Y$ 实际上是 $T^*Y$ 的仿射形变的情形。此时在每个奇点 $u_i$ 处，我们仍可定义留数 $\sigma_i^*$；确实，仿射形变局部地平凡，尽管没有典范方式使其平凡化，但平凡化的改变不影响留数。仿射形变情形的新特点是留数之和不再为零。代之以有

[begin equation]
(4.20)
\sum_i\sigma_i^*-\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}=0.

[end equation]

（推导这一结果的一种方式是注意到：我们可以在任意选定的点 $r\in Y$ 之外平凡化 $\zeta$。相对于这一平凡化，$D$ 多出一个奇点，留数为 $-\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}$，而式 (4.20) 不过是所有留数之和为零。）（留数之和为非零常数，对应于单值绝对值之积为不等于 1 的常数，事实上等于 $\exp(2\pi\,\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta_{\mathbb{C}})$。）

这解决了 §4.5 末尾提到的疑难。在那次计算中，我们用了 $Y=\mathcal{M}$ 上的坐标 $z$ 与 $\widetilde{T}^*\mathcal{M}$ 纤维上的坐标 $v$。这些坐标的选择在 $z=\infty$ 之外平凡化了仿射形变。特别地，形变在 $D$ 的极点附近被平凡化，因而没有影响那些极点的留数。在这一平凡化下，$D$ 在 $z=\infty$ 处还有一个极点，自然，包括最后这个极点的所有留数之和为零。

在微分几何中，可以把 $\zeta$ 表示为闭 $(1,1)$-形式（脚注：当然，在曲线上每个 $(1,1)$-形式都是闭的，但这一构造在更高维时确实有意义，如 §4.9 所讨论。}，并把这一情形描述如下。$\pi:\widetilde{T}^*Y\to Y$ 的亚纯截面 $D$ 并不对应于亚纯微分 $\omega$，而是对应于 $Y$ 上的 $(1,0)$-形式，具有如上极点，并满足

[begin equation]
(4.21)
\bar\partial\varphi=\zeta.

[end equation]

对此式在 $C$ 上积分便回到式 (4.20)；积分左端时使用 $\varphi$ 的极点，并把右端的积分用 $\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}$ 表示。
