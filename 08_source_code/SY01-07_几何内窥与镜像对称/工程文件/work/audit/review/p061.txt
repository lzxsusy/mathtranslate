the connected component of this group.) We write P for the Prym and T for its torsor
of line bundles L′ → D ′ obeying det π∗′ (L′ ) = O(p1 + · · · + p2g−2 ).

take any points si ∈ C, i = 1, . . . , k (allowing some of the points to coincide), denote
as s′i and s′′i the points in D ′ lying above si (with any choice of which one is which),
and define a line bundle N by

Conversely, one can show that every point of P can be represented by a line bundle of
this form. To see that (we thank T. Pantev for showing us this argument), let E be
a divisor on D ′ such that N = O(E). Then Nm(N ) = OC (π ′ (E)). If N ∈ P, then
π ′ (E) = (f ), the divisor of a rational function f on C. But it follows from Tsen’s
theorem that the norm map from the field of rational functions on D ′ to the field of
rational functions on C is surjective.30 Hence there exists a rational function g on D′
whose norm is f . Then π ′ ((g)) = (f ). Let E ′ = D − (g). Then π ′ (E ′ ) = 0 and therefore

E ′ has the form ki=1 (s′i − s′′i ). Thus, we obtain eqn. (5.12).
     Now let us find the connected components of the Prym. Obviously, the part of P
that we can construct for fixed k is connected, since C itself is connected and the points
si may move freely. Line bundles that differ by an exchange s′i ↔ s′′i lie in the same
connected component of P, since these two points are exchanged under monodromy
of si . Line bundles that differ by changing k by a multiple of 2 are also in the same
connected component; k is reduced by 2 if we take 2 of the si to be equal, with the
points labeled s′i and s′′i chosen properly, and use the identity (s′i − s′′i ) + (s′′i − s′i ) = 0.
     On the other hand, P actually has two connected components. This is shown in
[Mum], and also follows by a purely topological argument (see the discussion of eqn.
(5.15)).31 The argument in the last paragraph shows that the only possible invariant
is the value of k modulo 2. So it must be that the component of P connected to the
identity is characterized by even k, while the disconnected component is characterized
by odd k.

### CHINESE TRANSLATION

给定这样的线丛 $\mathcal{L}$，设 $\mathcal{L}'$ 为其到 $D'$ 的拉回。则 $\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{L}') = \mathcal{O}(p_1+\cdots+p_{2g-2})$。（当 $\mathcal{L}$ 提升到 $D'$ 时，我们放弃「截面在 $p_i$ 上方的点 $p'_i$ 与 $p''_i$ 处必须取相同值」这一要求，这导致所述结果。）这样的线丛构成的空间是（脚注：这句话的意思是：若 $\mathcal{L}'$ 与 $\mathcal{L}''$ 是两个线丛，且 $\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{L}') = \det\pi'_*(\mathcal{L}'') = \mathcal{O}(p_1+\cdots+p_{2g-2})$，则 $\mathcal{L}'' = \mathcal{L}'\otimes\mathcal{N}$，其中 $\mathcal{N}$ 唯一且满足 $\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{N}) = \mathcal{O}$。} 线丛群 $\mathcal{N}\to D'$（满足 $\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{N}) = \mathcal{O}$）的一个「torsor」。由于 $\pi': D'\to C$ 非分歧，$\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{N})$ 与 $\mathrm{Nm}(\mathcal{N})$ 相同，后者是 $\mathcal{N}$ 的（脚注：对任何曲线映射 $\pi: D\to C$，范数是从 $D$ 上的线丛到 $C$ 上的线丛的映射，定义如下。对整数 $n_i$ 与点 $p_i\in D$，$\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{O}(\sum_i n_i p_i)\to D$ 的范数定义为 $\mathrm{Nm}(\mathcal{N}) = \mathcal{O}(\sum_i n_i \pi(p_i))$。}「范数」。平凡范数的线丛群称为 $\pi': D'\to C$ 的 Prym 簇。（有时「Prym 簇」一词被用来指这个群的连通分支。）我们把 Prym 记作 $\boldsymbol{P}$，把满足 $\det\pi'_*(\mathcal{L}') = \mathcal{O}(p_1+\cdots+p_{2g-2})$ 的线丛 $\mathcal{L}'\to D'$ 所构成的 torsor 记作 $\boldsymbol{T}$。

我们可以容易地构造 $D'$ 上平凡范数线丛的一大族。取任意点 $s_i\in C$，$i=1,\dots,k$（允许某些点重合），把 $D'$ 中位于 $s_i$ 之上的点记作 $s'_i$ 与 $s''_i$（哪一个记作哪一个可任意选取），并如下定义线丛 $\mathcal{N}$：

[begin equation]
(5.12)
\mathcal{N} = \bigotimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}(s'_i - s''_i).

[end equation]

反之，可以证明 $\boldsymbol{P}$ 的每一点都可由这种形式的线丛表示。为看清这一点（我们要感谢 T. Pantev 向我们展示这一论证），设 $E$ 是 $D'$ 上的除子，使得 $\mathcal{N} = \mathcal{O}(E)$。则 $\mathrm{Nm}(\mathcal{N}) = \mathcal{O}_C(\pi'(E))$。若 $\mathcal{N}\in\boldsymbol{P}$，则 $\pi'(E) = (f)$，即 $C$ 上有理函数 $f$ 的除子。但由 Tsen 定理，从 $D'$ 上有理函数域到 $C$ 上有理函数域的范数映射是满的。于是存在 $D'$ 上的有理函数 $g$，其范数为 $f$。则 $\pi'((g)) = (f)$。令 $E' = D-(g)$。则 $\pi'(E') = 0$，因此 $E'$ 具有 $\sum_{i=1}^{k}(s'_i-s''_i)$ 的形式。这样就得到式 (5.12)。

现在我们来找出 Prym 的连通分支。显然，对固定 $k$ 能构造出的 $\boldsymbol{P}$ 的那一部分是连通的，因为 $C$ 本身连通且点 $s_i$ 可以自由移动。相差一次交换 $s'_i\leftrightarrow s''_i$ 的线丛位于 $\boldsymbol{P}$ 的同一连通分支，因为这两点在 $s_i$ 的单值作用下被交换。相差把 $k$ 改变 2 的倍数的线丛也位于同一连通分支；若取两个 $s_i$ 相等、把点适当标记为 $s'_i$ 与 $s''_i$，并利用恒等式 $(s'_i-s''_i)+(s''_i-s'_i) = 0$，$k$ 就减少 2。

另一方面，$\boldsymbol{P}$ 实际上有两个连通分支。这在 [Mum] 中被证明，也可由纯拓扑的论证得出（参见对式 (5.15) 的讨论）。（脚注：另一个证明见 [N1] 第 11 节。}上一段的论证表明，唯一可能的不变量是 $k$ 模 2 的值。因此必定是：$\boldsymbol{P}$ 中与恒等元相连的分支由偶数 $k$ 刻画，而不连通分支由奇数 $k$ 刻画。
