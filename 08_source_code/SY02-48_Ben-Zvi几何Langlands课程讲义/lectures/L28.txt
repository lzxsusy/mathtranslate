Parabolic induction and restriction: 5/6/21
We’ve been discussing the geometric Satake theorem. This theorem is about the category of G(O)-
equivariant sheaves on the aﬃne Grassmannian (or sheaves on G(O)\G(K)/G(O)), which is a semisimple
abelian category with a symmetric monoidal structure. With a ﬁber functor to Vect, we would have a
Tannakian category and therefore obtain a category of representations of a reductive group. This is what we
will discuss today, in the framework of parabolic induction and restriction. This is a framework for relating
representations of G and a maximal torus T.
Let G be a complex reductive group, such as GLn(C), SLn(C), and so on. We study ﬁnite-dimensional
G-representations through highest weight theory. A Borel subgroup B ⊂G is a maximal subgroup such that
G/B is a projective varietiy. Choose a Borel subgroup B; G/B is called the ﬂag variety of G. The standard
choice of B for G = GLn(C) is the subgroup of upper triangular matrices. The maximal torus, deﬁned as
a subgroup of G, is also a quotient of the Borel by a subgroup N (for the standard Borel, the subgroup of
strictly upper triangular matrices). So we have a correspondence

The point is that we have a functor SphG →SphT ≃RepT ∨described by taking a sheaf F, doing shriek-
restriction to N(K)-orbits, and taking cohomology. This is push-pull across (28.3). We therefore obtain a
Λ-graded vector space. Forgetting the grading, we obtain a functor SphG →Vect, and this is the ﬁber functor.
The extra structure that’s been ﬂoating around, such as the Λ-grading, allows one to show the ﬁber functor
is symmetric monoidal; it’s also pretty manifestly faithful, which can be seen by looking at orbits. So we can
run Tannakian representation.
Theorem 28.5 (Geometric Satake). As a symmetric monoidal category, (SphG, ∗) is equivalent to (RepG∨, ⊗),
where G∨is the automorphisms of the ﬁber functor, and is precisely the Langlands dual group of G.
The history is convoluted: Lusztig ﬁrst proved a result like this, and Ginzburg built further upon it.
Drinfeld produced the factorization picture, which is crucial, and Mirković-Vilonen assembled the pieces into
this theorem.
The Langlands dual group G∨is a reductive group whose maximal torus is T ∨, the dual to T. This
is because we produced a functor SphG →RepT ∨: Tannakian reconstruction tells us that this produces a
canonical subgroup T ∨⊂G∨: we didn’t have to choose it this time. One can check that the Weyl group
for G∨is isomorphic to that of G, and the root data for G and G∨are dual. It is also true that Z(G∨) is
identiﬁed with the Cartier dual of π1(G) = π0(GrG): on connected components, this construction recovers
Cartier duality.

There’s the question of what ﬁeld we’re working over. The answer is that we obtain G∨as a group over the
ﬁeld of coeﬃcients of our sheaves in the spherical category — we have been working over C, but we didn’t need
to: Mirković-Vilonen showed you can take arbitrary coeﬃcients, even Z, and G∨becomes deﬁned over that
coeﬃcient ring R, because you’re doing Tannakian reconstruction over R. This is a big deal: representations
of reductive groups over ﬁelds which are not algebraically closed can be very complicated. We previously
thought of the geometric Satake theorem as using representation theory (easier over algebraically closed ﬁelds
of characteristic zero) to understand the spherical category (harder), but over other coeﬃcient rings (e.g. in
modular characteristic), the theorem is still true even if the categories aren’t semisimple, and people use the
spherical category to make progress on understanding RepG. The ﬂow goes the other way!
Remark 28.6. “Any coeﬃcients?” What about over S? The spherical category still makes sense in homotopy
theory, where we just take sheaves of spectra, but there’s a big issue, where this category is no longer
symmetric monoidal: it’s only E3, and in derived settings, E3 is not E∞. It is known what can be done for a
derived geometric Satake theorem, but a lot of things are weird: you can’t even deﬁne a good determinant
map, hence SLn, over S.
