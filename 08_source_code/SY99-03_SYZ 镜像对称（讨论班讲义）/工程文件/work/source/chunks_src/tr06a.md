## Toric manifolds: complex & symplectic.

<<B191 p>>
Let $N$ be a lattice and $M$ its dual lattice, and write $\langle -,-\rangle\colon M\times N\to\mathbb{Z}$ the evaluation pairing.

<<B192 env>>
[ENV] **Definition.** A $k$-dimensional _regular cone_ is $\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}$ with generators $\langle v_1,\dotsb, v_k\rangle_{\geq\mathbb{R}}=\sigma$. A _face_ of $\sigma$ is $\langle v_{i_1},\dotsb, v_{i_r}\rangle_{\geq\mathbb{R}}$. $1$-dimensional cones are called _rays_.

<<B193 env>>
[ENV] **Definition.** A _fan_ is $\Sigma=\{\text{regular cones}\}$ such that

<<B194 li>>
- For all $\sigma\in\Sigma$, any faces of $\sigma$ lives in $\Sigma$;

<<B195 li>>
- For given $\sigma,\sigma'\in\Sigma$, the intersection $\sigma\cap\sigma'$ is a face of both $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$.

<<B196 p>>
$\Sigma$ is _complete_ if $N_{\mathbb{R}}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma$. Write $\Sigma^{(k)}=\{\text{all }k\text{-dimensional cones in }\Sigma\}$, and $G(\Sigma)=\{\text{generators of rays}\}$.

<<B197 env>>
[ENV] **Definition.** A collection $P\subseteq G(\Sigma)$ is _primitive_ if $P$ does not form a cone, yet any proper subset of $P$ does. Write $P(\Sigma)=\{\text{all primitive collections}\}$.

<<B198 env>>
[ENV] **Example.** Let $G(\Sigma)=\{e_1,\dotsb,e_n,-e_1-\dotsb-e_n\}$. Any collection which is not $G(\Sigma)$ generates a cone, so $G(\Sigma)$ is primitive.

<<B199 p>>
Consider $\mathbb{C}^N$ with coordinates $z_1,\dotsb, z_N$ corresponding to vectors $v_1,\dotsb, v_N$ in $G(\Sigma)$.

<<B200 env>>
[ENV] **Definition.** For a primitive collection $P$, write $\mathbb{A}(P)=\{(z_1,\dotsb, z_N)\colon z_i=0\text{ if } v_i\in P\}$.

<<B201 p>>
In the previous example, $\mathbb{A} (G(\Sigma))=\{(0,0,\dotsb, 0)\}$ and the quotient $\left(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\mathbb{A} (G(\Sigma))\right)\big/\mathbb{C}^{\times}\cong\mathbb{P}^n$. Write $Z(\Sigma)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{P\in P(\Sigma)}\mathbb{A}(P)$ and $U(\Sigma)=\mathbb{C}^N\setminus Z(\Sigma)$. The assignment $e_i\mapsto v_i$ gives a group homomorphism $\pi\colon\mathbb{Z}^N\to\mathbb{Z}^n$ with kernel $\mathbb{K}=\ker (\pi)$. Each vector $\vec{\lambda} =(\lambda_1,\dotsb,\lambda_N)\in\mathbb{K}$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^N$ by $a_{\vec{\lambda}}(t).(z_1,\dotsb, z_N)=(t^{\lambda_1}z_1,\dotsb, t^{\lambda_N}z_N)$. Let $D(\Sigma)=\langle a_{\vec{\lambda}}\colon\vec{\lambda}\in\mathbb{K}\rangle\subseteq\left(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)^N$ be the algebraic torus generated by $\mathbb{K}$.

<<B202 env>>
[ENV] **Lemma.** $D(\Sigma)$ acts freely on $U(\Sigma)$.

<<B203 p>>
The _toric variety_ associated to the fan $\Sigma$ is defined to be $X_{\Sigma} =U(\Sigma)/D(\Sigma)$. In the previous example, the map $\pi$ is given by $\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{Z}^n\to 0$ where $e_i\mapsto e_i$ for $1\leq i\leq n$ and $e_{n+1}\mapsto -e_1-\dotsb -e_n$, so that $\mathbb{K} =\langle e_1+\dotsb +e_n\rangle$, and we get the action $a_{\lambda}(t).(z_1,\dotsb, z_n)=(t^{\lambda}z_1,\dotsb, t^{\lambda}z_n)$ is the usual diagonal action for $\mathbb{P}^n$.

<<B204 env>>
[ENV] **Definition.** For a regular cone $\sigma=\langle v_{i_1},\dotsb,v_{i_r}\rangle$, write $U_{\sigma}=\{(z_1,\dotsb,z_N)\in\mathbb{C}^N\vert z_j\neq 0\text{ for }j\not\in\{i_1,\dotsb, i_r\}\}$.

<<B205 p>>
For $\mathbb{P}^n$, we get $U_i=\{z_i\neq 0\}$.

<<B206 env>>
[ENV] **Proposition.** (1) $U(\Sigma)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\sigma} U(\sigma)$, (2) $U(\sigma)\supseteq U(\sigma')$ if $\sigma$ is a face of $\sigma'$, (3) $U(\sigma_1\cap\sigma_2)=U(\sigma_1)\cap U(\sigma_2)$.

<<B207 env>>
[ENV] **Proposition.** $\sigma=\langle v_1,\dotsb, v_n\rangle$ with dual basis $\{u_{i_1},\dotsb, u_{i_n}\}$, then $U(\sigma)\cong\mathbb{C}^n\times\left(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)^{N-n}$ where $U_{\sigma}=U(\sigma)/D(\Sigma)\cong\mathbb{C}^n$ with$$\begin{array}{c} x_1^{\sigma}=z_1^{\langle v,u_{i_1}\rangle}\dotsb z_N^{\langle v_N,u_{i_1}\rangle}\\ \vdots\\ x_n^{\sigma}=z_1^{\langle v_1,u_{i_n}\rangle}\dotsb z_N^{\langle v_N,u_{i_n}\rangle} \end{array}$$

## Sympletic aspects.

<<B209 p>>
Consider the polytope $P=\{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\langle x,v_i\rangle\geq\lambda_i\}$. For a face $\Gamma$, write $\sigma_{\Gamma}\colon =\displaystyle\bigcup_{r\geq 0} r\cdot (P -m)$ where $m\in\mathrm{int}\ \Gamma$. We then obtain the _dual cone_ $\check{\sigma}_{\Gamma} =\{x\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\langle y,x\rangle\geq 0\ \forall y\in\sigma_{\Gamma}\}$ and an induced complete fan $\Sigma (P)$. The algebraic torus action of $\left(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)^N$ gives a symplectic action of the real torus $T^N$ on $U(\Sigma (P))$, and hence we get the moment map $\mu\colon U(\Sigma (P))\to T_e^{\ast} T^N\ni r$, so that the _symplectic toric manifold_ is the symplectic reduction $\mu^{-1} (r)/T^N=X_{\Sigma(P)}$ together with the quotient symplectic structure.

## Morse-Bott Floer cohomology.

<<B211 p>>
In this section we discuss a Morse-Bott version of Floer cohomology for Lagrangian submanifolds. Let $M$ be a smoothmanifold.

<<B212 env>>
[ENV] **"Definition".** A _geometric chain_ of dimension $k$ is a simplicial complex $P$ of dimension $k$ with $P=P_{reg}\cup P_S$ where $P_{reg}$ is a $k$-dimensional oriented submanifold together with some conditions, and $f\colon P\to M$ continuous function such that $f\vert_{P_{reg}}$ is smooth.

<<B213 p>>
For a geometric chain $[P,f]$, we get a function $$\begin{array}{rcl}T_{[P,f]}\colon\Omega_c^k (M)&\to &\mathbb{R}\\ \alpha&\mapsto&\int_{P_{reg}} f^{\ast}\alpha \end{array}$$Define differential $\partial T_{[P,f]} (\alpha) =T_{[P,f]} (d\alpha)$. This gives the cohomology $H_{Sing}^{\ast} (M)$. If $[P,f]$ is such that $\dim f(P)<\dim P$, then $[P,f]$ is the zero map on $\Omega^{\ast} (M)$.

<<B214 p>>
Now consider the pair $(M,L)$ where $M$ is a symplectic manifold and $L\subseteq M$ a Lagrangian submanifold of $M$. For $\beta\in\pi_2 (M,L)$, we can define $$\mathrm{Aut}\ (D)\curvearrowright\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_n (L,J;\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}{l}w\colon (\mathbb{D},\partial\mathbb{D};z_1,\dotsb, z_n)\to (M,L)\\ \bar{\partial}_Jw=0\end{array}\right\}$$ and the quotient $\mathcal{M}_n =\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_n/\mathrm{Aut}\ (D)$ admits a compactification $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_n$.
